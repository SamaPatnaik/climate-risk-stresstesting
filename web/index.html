Terms used on this page

PD (probability of default)
The chance a borrower stops paying in a given year. Here it starts at BC's observed 90-day arrears rate, 0.26%.
LGD (loss given default)
The share of the loan the lender loses once a borrower defaults, after selling the home. Insured mortgages pass most of this loss to the insurer.
EAD (exposure at default)
How much is still owed on the loan. Summed across loans, it is the size of the book.
Expected loss (EL)
PD × LGD × EAD: what the lender loses in a typical year, averaged over many years.
ES 99% (expected shortfall)
The average loss in the worst 1% of simulated years. It describes a bad year rather than a typical one.
Uplift
How much higher a number is under a warmer scenario than under historical climate, in dollars or as a percentage.
Hazard score
How much of a division's land burns in a year, scaled from 0 to 1. A score of 1 would be twice the worst division-year on record.
Scenario multiplier
A single number that scales every division's hazard to stand in for a warmer climate: 1.0 is historical, 2.0 is Hot House.

How the model works

  1. Hazard

    For each census division and each fire season from 1990 to 2025, we measure the share of land inside BC Wildfire Service fire perimeters. That share becomes the hazard score h. A scenario multiplies it by m, capped at 1: H = min(1, h × m).

  2. Portfolio

    We build 100,000 synthetic mortgages and place them across divisions in proportion to the number of owner households with a mortgage in the 2021 Census. Balances, insurance status and home values are drawn at random.

  3. Loss model

    Hazard raises both the chance of default and, for uninsured loans, the loss when it happens: PD = PD0(1 + βPDH), LGD = LGD0 + βLGDH, EL = PD × LGD × EAD. PD0 is set so that under historical climate the BC default rate equals observed arrears.

  4. Simulation

    We run 1,000 simulated years. Each one replays a randomly chosen historical fire season, draws the two βs and decides which loans default. Every scenario uses the same draws, so the differences between them come from the multiplier alone. BC tail measures come from the BC total in each run, never from adding up division tails.

Limitations

  • Burned land is not where homes are. Hazard is the share of a division's whole area that burns, and most homes sit in valleys and towns that burn less often than the forest around them.
  • The βs and the scenario multipliers are judgment calls, not estimates from data. The multipliers borrow NGFS scenario names but do not come from NGFS models.
  • ES 99% rests on about 10 runs out of 1,000, and those runs are dominated by a few fire seasons. One more or one fewer extreme season in the record would move it noticeably.
  • Every home is treated as equally vulnerable. Building type, defensible space and distance to the fire edge are not modelled.
